# Fact sheet: Brooklyn ghosts

Researched Nov 21 2026.

Deroy, the fact sheet is below. Four things to know before you use it:

- **Green-Wood check:** Ebbets, Chadwick, Beecher, Clinton, Morse and McKinney Steward are all buried at Green-Wood. Chisholm, Horne, Whitman, Biggie and RBG are not.
- **Chisholm monument:** it isn't built yet. Ground was broken in March 2026, and the plaza with the monument is the last phase of that work.
- **Coordinates:** I worked these out from the street addresses, accurate to roughly 50 m. I didn't check them on a map, so check them before you place the ghosts.
- **Quotes:** I've included only three, all checked word for word. People without one are marked "none".

---

**Shirley Chisholm (1924–2005)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** Born in Brooklyn; sources don't name the neighborhood. She went to Girls' High in Bed-Stuy, was active in the Bed-Stuy Democratic club, and owned a house on St. Johns Place.
- **Buried:** Forest Lawn Cemetery, Buffalo NY. Not Green-Wood. Sources disagree on which mausoleum (Birchwood or Oakwood).
- **Facts:**
  - In 1968 she became the first Black woman elected to Congress.
  - In 1972 she was the first Black candidate to run for a major party's presidential nomination.
  - From age 5 to 9 she lived in Barbados.
- **Quote:** "Unbought and Unbossed", her campaign slogan, which is carved on her vault.
- **Ghost spot:** Ocean Ave & Parkside Ave entrance to Prospect Park, the future monument plaza: 40.6553, -73.9617. If you'd rather use a finished place, Shirley Chisholm State Park: 40.6455, -73.8720.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Chisholm), [NPS](https://home.nps.gov/people/shirley-chisholm.htm), [DOT 2026](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2026/nyc-dot-prospect-park-alliance.shtml), [Prospect Park Alliance](https://www.prospectpark.org/city-approves-vale-and-chisholm-monument/)

**Dr. Susan Smith McKinney Steward (1846 or 1847–1918)**
- **Birth year:** Green-Wood and Find a Grave say 1846. NYC Parks and Wikipedia say March 1847.
- **Brooklyn tie:** Born in Weeksville, the free Black community. She grew up on her father's farm at Fulton St & Buffalo Ave.
- **Buried:** Green-Wood, yes.
- **Facts:**
  - In 1870 she became New York State's first Black woman doctor, finishing first in her class at New York Medical College for Women.
  - In 1881 she co-founded the Brooklyn Woman's Homeopathic Hospital at Myrtle and Grand Avenues.
  - She specialized in prenatal care and childhood diseases, and she supported women's suffrage.
- **Quote:** none.
- **Ghost spot:** Weeksville Heritage Center, 158 Buffalo Ave: 40.6741, -73.9256.
- Sources: [Green-Wood](https://www.green-wood.com/susan-smith-mckinney-steward/), [NYC Parks](https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/susan-smith-mckinney-steward-park/)

**Lena Horne (1917–2010)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** Born in Bed-Stuy. She lived at 519 Macon St until about age 5, and was later raised by her grandparents at 189 Chauncey St.
- **Buried:** Cremated, with the ashes given to family. Not Green-Wood. I couldn't find where the ashes went.
- **Facts:**
  - She spoke and performed at the 1963 March on Washington.
  - She worked with Eleanor Roosevelt for anti-lynching laws.
  - She broke with the wartime USO after Black soldiers were seated behind German prisoners of war.
- **Quote:** none.
- **Ghost spot:** Lena Horne Bandshell in Prospect Park, renamed for her in June 2021: 40.6669, -73.9787.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_Horne), [Prospect Park Alliance](https://www.prospectpark.org/prospect-park-bandshell-renamed-lena-horne/), [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/52189627/lena-horne)

**Walt Whitman (1819–1892)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** Edited the Brooklyn Daily Eagle from 1846 to 1848. The first edition of Leaves of Grass came out in 1855, when he lived in Brooklyn.
- **Fort Greene Park campaign:** Yes, he campaigned for it.
  - The City of Brooklyn set the site aside as a park in 1845.
  - He wrote about it almost daily in 1846.
  - In 1847 he published the editorial "Fort Greene Park—And How We Shall Be Able To Get It," arguing that workers needed outdoor space.
  - Also in 1847, the state legislature approved buying the land for Washington Park.
  - Sources disagree on when it opened: 1848 or 1849. The park's own inscription says it "opened 1849 through efforts of Walt Whitman."
- **Buried:** Harleigh Cemetery, Camden NJ, in a tomb he designed. Not Green-Wood.
- **Quote:** none (the editorial title above is not a quote).
- **Ghost spot:** Fort Greene Park: 40.6916, -73.9755.
- Sources: [NYC Parks](https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/fort-greene-park/monuments/1222&lang=en), [Wikipedia: Fort Greene Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Greene_Park), [Whitman Archive](https://whitmanarchive.org/item/encyclopedia_entry474)

**Charles Ebbets (1859–1925)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** Owned the Brooklyn Dodgers. Starting in 1908 he quietly bought up lots in "Pigtown," Flatbush, an area now counted as Crown Heights.
- **Buried:** Green-Wood, yes, on a hill above Chadwick's grave.
- **Facts:**
  - Ebbets Field opened in April 1913. Sources give April 5 (an exhibition game) or April 9 (the first league game).
  - The Dodgers played there until 1957, and it was torn down in 1960.
  - The site became the Ebbets Field Apartments, affordable housing for about 1,300 families.
- **Quote:** none.
- **Ghost spot:** 1720 Bedford Ave: 40.6651, -73.9582.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebbets_Field), [Brownstoner](https://www.brownstoner.com/history/past-and-present-ebbets-field-crown-heights/)

**Henry Chadwick (1824–1908)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** Brooklyn sportswriter. His first box score, in 1859, covered the Brooklyn Excelsiors against the Brooklyn Stars.
- **Buried:** Green-Wood, yes. His monument has a granite baseball and bases at the corners.
- **Facts:**
  - He invented the box score and the "K" for a strikeout.
  - He's credited with batting average and earned run average, a good hook for "data helps us see clearly."
  - He was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1938.
- **Quote:** none.
- **Ghost spot:** Green-Wood main gate (5th Ave & 25th St): 40.6584, -73.9944.
- Sources: [SABR](https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/henry-chadwick/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Chadwick_(writer))

**Henry Ward Beecher (1813–1887)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** First pastor of Plymouth Church, Orange St, Brooklyn Heights, from 1847.
- **Buried:** Green-Wood, yes.
- **Facts:**
  - Plymouth Church was called the "Grand Central Depot" of the Underground Railroad. Most of that history comes from oral accounts.
  - On February 5, 1860, his congregation raised money to free 9-year-old Sally Maria Diggs, called "Pinky." Sources disagree on the amount.
  - Abraham Lincoln worshipped there in 1860.
- **His later scandal, stated neutrally:** In 1875 a former friend sued him, accusing him of adultery. The trial ended with the jury unable to agree.
- **Quote:** none.
- **Ghost spot:** Plymouth Church: 40.6990, -73.9932.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Church_(Brooklyn)), [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/in-search-of-the-underground-railroad-in-brooklyn), [BPL packet](https://static.bklynlibrary.org/prod/public/documents/Plymouth%20Church%20NEW.pdf)

**DeWitt Clinton (1769–1828)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** His burial only. His remains were moved to Green-Wood in 1844, which helped make the cemetery nationally known. Sources disagree on the year (1844 or 1853); the search results I used say 1844.
- **Buried:** Green-Wood, yes. His grave has a bronze statue by Henry Kirke Brown.
- **Facts:**
  - He was Mayor of New York City, a US Senator, and Governor of New York (two separate terms).
  - He championed the Erie Canal.
- **Quote:** none.
- **Ghost spot:** Green-Wood, at the gate given above (I don't have exact coordinates for his grave).
- Source: [Green-Wood list](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Burials_at_Green-Wood_Cemetery)

**Samuel F. B. Morse (1791–1872)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** His burial only. I found no record of him living or working in Brooklyn.
- **Buried:** Green-Wood, yes. A plaque from the Morse Telegraph Club on his grave was stolen and came back in 2012.
- **Facts:**
  - He was a painter before he was an inventor.
  - He developed Morse code with Alfred Vail.
  - He sent the first long-distance telegraph message, from Washington to Baltimore, in 1844, with $30,000 from Congress.
- **Quote:** "What hath God wrought!", the first message, 1844.
- **Ghost spot:** Green-Wood, at the gate given above.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Morse), [BPL](https://www.bklynlibrary.org/blog/2019/03/06/dedication-bronze-plaque)

**The Notorious B.I.G. (Christopher Wallace, 1972–1997)**
- **Brooklyn tie:** Yes, St. James Place. He grew up at 226 St. James Place, Clinton Hill, in a third-floor apartment with his mother, Voletta. The block between Fulton St and Gates Ave was named "Christopher 'Notorious B.I.G.' Wallace Way" in 2019.
- **Death:** Killed in a drive-by shooting in Los Angeles on March 9, 1997, at age 24.
- **Buried:** Cremated, with the ashes given to family. Not Green-Wood. I couldn't find where the ashes are.
- **Facts:**
  - A fan and advocate started the street-naming push in 2013, which shows how residents can get a place renamed.
  - Some community board members opposed it.
  - His funeral procession went through his old neighborhood.
- **Quote:** none, and no lyrics.
- **Ghost spot:** St. James Pl & Fulton St: 40.6828, -73.9634.
- Sources: [6sqft](https://www.6sqft.com/the-bed-stuy-block-where-notorious-b-i-g-grew-up-has-officially-been-named-after-the-famous-rapper/), [Biography](https://www.biography.com/musicians/biggie-smalls)

**Ruth Bader Ginsburg (1933–2020)**
- **Born:** Brooklyn, at Beth Moses Hospital in Borough Park (now Maimonides). Her parents lived in Flatbush.
- **Grew up:** 1584 East 9th St, Midwood. She went to P.S. 238, then James Madison High School (class of 1950).
- **Buried:** Arlington National Cemetery, next to her husband. Not Green-Wood.
- **Facts:**
  - She was a Supreme Court Justice.
  - Before that, in the 1970s, she argued cases to open doors that had been closed to women.
  - She has a statue at City Point in Downtown Brooklyn. I didn't confirm its location.
- **Quote:** "Fight for the things that you care about, but do it in a way that will lead others to join you." Radcliffe Medal, May 29, 2015.
- **Ghost spot:** James Madison High School, 3787 Bedford Ave: 40.6094, -73.9480.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Bader_Ginsburg), [Untapped](https://www.untappedcities.com/ruth-bader-ginsburg-childhood-home-brooklyn/), [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2015/05/honoring-ruth-bader-ginsburg/)
