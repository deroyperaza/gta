# Fact sheet: transit equity, food access, Crown Heights mediation, politics

Researched Nov 21 2026 for missions 14–16. Every line has its source. Flags mark what is uncertain.

I verified most of the sheet against official or reputable sources. A few items are still uncertain and flagged inline. Two things changed recently: Fair Fares expands to 200% of the poverty level on Nov 30, 2026, and Health Bucks rises to a $14/day cap in Jan 2027.

## Mission A: Transit equity

**1. Fair Fares NYC**
- **Age and residency:** riders must be 18–64 and live in one of the five boroughs. Immigration status doesn't matter. People getting full carfare from DSS/HRA, or a reduced fare through another NYC program, can't get the subway/bus discount. — https://www.nyc.gov/site/fairfares/index.page
- **Income limit (recent change):** it rises from 150% to 200% of the federal poverty level on **Nov 30, 2026**, under the FY27 budget deal (June 30, 2026). That's about $31,920 for one person and $66,000 for a family of four, and about 340,000 more people become eligible. Earlier steps: 100% at launch, 120% in 2023, 145% in 2024, 150% in 2025. — https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/traffic_and_transit/2026/06/30/city-50--off-transit-program-expands-eligibility · https://secretnyc.co/fair-fares-nyc-expansion-income-limits-how-to-apply/
  - FLAG: as of this week nyc.gov still shows the old 150% table ($23,940 for one person).
- **Discount:** 50% off, so $1.50 a ride, with a weekly cap of $17.50. It covers subway, "eligible" buses, the Staten Island Railway, the Roosevelt Island Tram and Access-A-Ride. Riders pick either the transit discount or the Access-A-Ride discount, not both. — nyc.gov/fairfares (above)
  - FLAG: official pages never say whether express buses are covered, and the phrase "eligible bus fares" suggests they aren't. Don't claim express bus coverage in the game.
- **How to apply:** online through ACCESS HRA (web or app) via nyc.gov/accessfairfares, or at one office per borough. Applicants have 10 days to upload documents. The card arrives within about 3 weeks, and enrollment renews yearly. — https://access.nyc.gov/programs/fair-fares/

**2. Fares**
- **Base fare:** $3 for subway, local bus and Access-A-Ride since Jan 4, 2026 (up from $2.90). Express bus is $7.25.
- **Fare cap:** after 12 paid rides in a rolling 7 days, the rest of the week is free, so no one pays more than $35. It only works if you tap the same card or phone every time.
- **MetroCard:** unlimited MetroCards are no longer sold.
- **Next increase:** another ~4% rise is expected in 2027 (not yet set).
- Sources: https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/news/2026/01/02/mta-fare-and-toll-hikes-to-take-effect-sunday · https://gothamist.com/news/nyc-subway-and-bus-fares-going-up-to-3-in-2026-mta-officials-say

**3. Transit deserts**
- **Official source:** the Borough President's 2025 Comprehensive Plan says much of southeast Brooklyn has no subway or ferry and limited bus service. — https://www.brooklynbp.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Comprehensive_Plan-1.pdf
- **Bergen Beach, Georgetown, Marine Park, Mill Basin, Mill Island:** no station inside the area. The nearest are Kings Highway (B/Q) and Flatbush Ave–Brooklyn College (2/5). — https://ht.hcr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/11/nyrcr-plan_southeast-brooklyn-waterfront-part-ii.pdf
- **Unserved by rapid transit (Regional Plan Association):** East Flatbush, Flatlands, Marine Park, Mill Basin and Sheepshead Bay. — http://fourthplan.org/action/new-subways
- FLAG: I didn't find a cited source for Gerritsen Beach, Canarsie or Red Hook. They're plausible, but source them before using them.

**4. Buses**
- **B44 SBS (Nostrand):** launched Nov 17, 2013. Peak trips got more than 30% faster. — https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2016/pr16-074.shtml
- **B46 SBS (Utica):** launched July 3, 2016, after DOT and the MTA presented to Community Boards 1, 3, 8, 9, 17 and 18 and set up a Community Advisory Committee. Merchant pushback cut bus-lane enforcement to 7am–7pm. That's a good model for how residents shape a project. — https://politicsny.com/2016/07/05/mta-rolls-b46-select-bus-service-utica-avenue/ · https://www.nyc.gov/html/brt/html/routes/utica.shtml
- **Flatbush Ave (new, Nov 17, 2026):** center-running bus lanes are open from Livingston St to Grand Army Plaza, which the city calls its first "BRT-style" corridor. Outreach has started on the next stretch, Grand Army Plaza to Avenue H. The goal is full bus rapid transit by 2030 under the city–state "Next Stop" plan. — https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/11/mayor-mamdani-delivers-faster-buses-on-flatbush-avenue--laying-g
  - FLAG: a full car-free busway is only an advocates' idea, not a plan.
- **Brooklyn Bus Network Redesign:** still "in progress." The draft came out Dec 2022, and the MTA Board hasn't voted on a final plan. — https://www.mta.info/project/bus-network-redesign
  - FLAG: I couldn't confirm whether a proposed final plan has come out.

**5. Dollar vans**
- **Fare:** most charge a flat $2, cash.
- **Licensing:** only 35 to 63 vans hold a TLC commuter-van license (35 in Oct 2024, 63 more recently; 215 in 2019), so most vans on Flatbush are unlicensed. A van without a TLC base number and license number is likely illegal.
- **Enforcement:** the TLC issued 113 summonses in 2025, up from 59 the year before.
- FLAG: there is no official count of the unlicensed share.
- Sources: https://gothamist.com/news/brooklyns-dollar-vans-squeezed-by-mamdanis-flatbush-avenue-bus-plan · https://home4.nyc.gov/assets/tlc/downloads/pdf/agency_hearing_testimon_9_15_25.pdf

## Mission B: Food access

1. Brownsville has about 1 supermarket for every 15 bodegas and East New York 1 for every 13, based on the city's Supermarket Need Index. "Food apartheid" is a term used by advocates such as East New York Farms, not the city. — https://brooklyncommunities.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Health-Housing-PAR-Report_2020-2021.pdf · https://nyassembly.gov/write/upload/publichearing/001501/006316.pdf
   - FLAG: these figures are from around 2018–2021.
2. **FRESH:**
   - **Zoning (City Planning):** an extra square foot of housing for every square foot of grocery store, up to 20,000.
   - **Tax (Industrial Development Agency/EDC):** up to 25 years of property-tax relief.
   - **Store rules:** at least 6,000 sq ft, half of it food, 30% of that perishables.
   - **Status:** 23 of the city's 59 community districts are eligible. The program was expanded in 2021, and 27 stores had used it as of 2024.
   - Sources: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/downloads/pdf/our-work/plans/citywide/food-retail-expansion-support-health-fresh/fresh-report-feb2023.pdf · https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024-10-Fiscal-Note-NYC-FRESH.pdf
3. **Health Bucks:** $2 for every $2 spent with SNAP/EBT, up to $10 a day, usable at all NYC farmers markets and farm stands year-round. — https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/health/health-topics/health-bucks.page
   - Recent change: the cap rises to $14 a day in Jan 2027. — https://pix11.com/news/morning/snap-changes-to-help-people-buy-more-produce/ (news only; not yet on nyc.gov)
4. **GrowNYC Fresh Food Box:** sliding scale of $14, $17 or $20, or $7 with SNAP. No income requirement. — https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/nyc-food-resource-guide-coney-island/ · https://grownyc.org/grow-your-snap/
   - FLAG: I didn't find a current list of Brooklyn sites.
5. **Community fridges:** there's no city permit or guidance written for them. They're tolerated rather than permitted. Health Code Article 81 requires a permit for charities serving food more than once a week, and food must be kept at 41°F or below. — https://chlpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Fridge-QA-FINAL.pdf · https://on.nyc.gov/article-81-health-code
   - FLAG: the legal gray zone itself is the accurate fact.
   - **Pushing for a grocery store:** testify at the community board, ask the Council member for funding, and point developers to FRESH. These steps are inferred from how the programs work, not taken from an official how-to.

## Politics layer

- **City Council terms:** 51 members serving 4-year terms, two-term limit. 2021 and 2023 were two-year terms after redistricting. The last election was Nov 4, 2025, and the next is Nov 6, 2029. Ranked-choice voting is used in primaries and special elections only, not general elections. Speaker: Julie Menin. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council · https://ballotpedia.org/New_York,_New_York
- **Community Boards:** 59 boards of up to 50 unpaid members. Borough presidents appoint them for 2-year terms, half nominated by Council members. They are advisory only: land use (ULURP), budget and city services. — https://home4.nyc.gov/site/cau/community-boards/about-commmunity-boards.page
- **Participatory budgeting:**
  - **The People's Money (4th cycle):** voting ran May 6–June 21, 2026, open to anyone 11 and up regardless of immigration status. $4M went to 20 projects at $200K each. 134,418 people voted, 31,952 of them in Brooklyn. — https://www.nyc.gov/site/civicengagement/news/nyc-cec-winning-projects-082726.page
  - **Separate Council PB:** about $30M across 24 districts, voting closed April 19, 2026. — https://council.nyc.gov/press/2025/05/30/2892

## Mission C: Crown Heights

1. **1991:**
   - On Aug 19, 1991, a car in the Lubavitcher Rebbe's motorcade went onto the sidewalk and killed 7-year-old Gavin Cato, the son of Guyanese immigrants, and badly injured his cousin Angela.
   - Hours later, Yankel Rosenbaum, a 29-year-old Australian graduate student, was stabbed by a group of young men and died. Lemrick Nelson was acquitted of murder but later convicted on federal civil-rights charges.
   - The driver was not indicted. A state investigation faulted the slow police response.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Heights_riot · https://www.baruch.cuny.edu/nycdata/disasters/riots-crown_heights.html
   - FLAG, contested: what to call it ("riot," "rebellion" or "pogrom") depends on who is speaking. The length is reported as 3 or 4 days. Stick to the facts above.
2. **Organizations:**
   - **Neighbors in Action** (formerly the Crown Heights Community Mediation Center, founded 1998, renamed 2018; part of the Center for Justice Innovation): Save Our Streets violence interruption, youth peer leaders, free legal help, placekeeping events. It is active in 2025 and won the 2024 Brooklyn Org Spark Prize. — https://www.innovatingjustice.org/program/neighbors-in-action/
     - FLAG: its current page no longer lists mediation or Black–Jewish dialogue.
   - **Project CARE / Crown Heights Youth Collective** (Richard Green): interfaith youth meetings and basketball after 1991. — http://brooklynink.org/2011/08/17/27262-twenty-years-after-the-riot-crown-heights%E2%80%99-blacks-and-jews-have-built-a-network-of-relationships/
     - FLAG: I couldn't confirm it's active in 2025–26.
   - **Shomrim/Shmira joint patrol work:** I found no current source. Don't use it.
3. **Free mediation:** the NY courts' Community Dispute Resolution Centers offer free or low-cost mediation, conflict coaching, restorative justice and large-group dialogue, whether or not you have a court case. Brooklyn's center is the New York Peace Institute's Brooklyn Mediation Center, (718) 834-6671, with volunteer mediators. — https://ww2.nycourts.gov/ip/adr/cdrc.shtml · https://nypeace.org/mediation/
   - FLAG: confirm its current address (345 Adams St is listed, but its own page says it's moving).
4. **Shared concerns:**
   - Both communities have publicly raised housing costs and displacement, tied to the Atlantic Ave rezoning and a 13-story development. — https://www.nycitynewsservice.com/2025/11/18/central-brooklyn-residents-voice-concerns-about-atlantic-avenue-rezoning-and-deepening-gentrification/ · https://collive.com/13-story-crown-heights-development-raises-questions-over-housing/
   - In 2019 leaders on both sides named affordability as the root issue. — https://wnyc.org/story/attacks-against-jews-crown-heights-stir-old-fears-and-new-dialogue/
   - FLAG: I found no recent statement the two communities made together. There is also a live tension: in 2025 an activist group blamed rising rents on "Zionists," and Black local officials condemned it. Handle with care.
