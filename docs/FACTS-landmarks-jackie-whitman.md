## A) Jackie Robinson
- **Broke the color barrier:** April 15, 1947, at Ebbets Field, playing first base for the Dodgers. https://www.mlb.com/jackie-robinson-day
- **Rookie of the Year:** 1947, the first year MLB gave the award. https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/robinson-jackie
- **1955 World Series:** the Dodgers beat the Yankees in 7 games, Brooklyn's only title. He stole home in Game 1 (Yogi Berra always insisted he was out). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_World_Series
- **Civil rights and business:**
  - NAACP board member, 1957 to 1967, and he led its Freedom Fund drive.
  - Vice president at Chock full o'Nuts, starting in 1957.
  - Co-founded **Freedom National Bank** in Harlem in 1964. Yes, your guess was right: it was a Black-owned bank.
  - Source: https://www.loc.gov/collections/jackie-robinson-baseball/articles-and-essays/baseball-the-color-line-and-jackie-robinson/
- **Death:** October 24, 1972, age 53, in Stamford, CT.
- **Burial:** Cypress Hills Cemetery, which straddles the Brooklyn/Queens line and is split by the Jackie Robinson Parkway. Section 6, near his son Jackie Jr. As noted at the top, the Find a Grave point falls in Queens; treat the exact spot as ⚠. https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/882/jackie-robinson
- **Ebbets Field:** the block bounded by Bedford Ave, Sullivan Pl, McKeever Pl and Montgomery St. So "Bedford & Sullivan" is correct as a corner. Demolition began February 23, 1960, and the apartments opened in 1962. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebbets_Field (40.665, -73.95806)
- **Quote:** "A life is not important except in the impact it has on other lives."
  - Your wording is the standard version.
  - Book source: *I Never Had It Made* (1972, with Alfred Duckett), excerpted in *Newsday*, Nov 5, 1972. I didn't read the book itself.
  - It's also carved on his gravestone; I couldn't confirm the stone's exact text.
  - https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Jackie_Robinson
- **Respect note:** his widow Rachel Robinson founded the Jackie Robinson Foundation. Keep the ghost's lines close to his documented words and avoid inventing opinions.

## B) Whitman and Fort Greene Park
- **Yes, he campaigned for it.** Whitman edited the Brooklyn Daily Eagle from March 1846 to January 1848 and wrote editorials urging the city to make the Fort Greene site a public park.
- **1846:** his poem "Ode: To be sung on Fort Greene; 4th of July 1846" ran in the Eagle on July 2, 1846. https://whitmanarchive.org/item/per.00062
- **1847:** the city created Washington Park. ⚠ Sources give the opening as 1847, 1848 or 1850.
- **Later:** Olmsted and Vaux redesigned the park in the 1860s, and it was renamed Fort Greene Park in 1897. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Greene_Park
- **Scholarly backup:** a 1994 article in the Walt Whitman Quarterly Review covers his 1846 campaign and a "Fort Greene Park" editorial. ⚠ Exact editorial dates not confirmed. https://pdf.core.ac.uk/corefilesystem/pdf/1635/61121121.pdf
