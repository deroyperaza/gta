# Fact sheet: older adults, environmental justice, racial justice

Researched Nov 22 2026. [V] verified on a primary source, [S] secondary, [!] check first.

I've built the fact sheet below. Two things changed after 2025 that would make older copy wrong. A March 2026 state court ruling put New York State's protection for voucher holders on hold, though NYC's own law still applies. And the Brooklyn peaker plants have been allowed to keep running until May 2029.

Each fact is marked **[V]** (I checked it on an official or primary source), **[S]** (secondary source only) or **[!]** (uncertain, check before shipping).

## A. Older adults

- **[V]** SCRIE eligibility: you must be 62 or older, with total household income for the prior year of $50,000 or less. All income counts, taxed or not. https://on.nyc.gov/SCRIE/
- **[V]** Rent must be more than one-third of monthly household income. The apartment must be rent stabilized, rent controlled, Mitchell-Lama or a Redevelopment Company building. No one in the household can already get DRIE, Section 8 or a similar benefit. https://access.nyc.gov/programs/senior-citizens-rent-increase-exemption-scrie/
- **[V]** What SCRIE does: it freezes rent at its current level and exempts the tenant from future legal increases. The landlord gets a property tax credit for the difference. https://www.nyc.gov/site/finance/property/landlords-scrie.page
- **[V]** How to apply: online through NYC TAP (nyc.gov/nyctap), by mail to NYC Dept. of Finance, or through 311 or the Rent Freeze Walk-In Center at 66 John St. Mitchell-Lama tenants apply through HPD instead. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/rentfreeze/downloads/pdf/scrie/scrie-application.pdf
- **[V]** NYC Aging runs 300+ Older Adult Centers (they used to be called "senior centers"). Membership is free for anyone 60+. Centers offer daily meals and activities such as arts, health programs, tech help and case assistance. https://www.nyc.gov/site/dfta/services/older-adult-center.page
- **[V]** To find a center: call Aging Connect at 212-AGING-NYC (212-244-6469), or call 311. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dfta/downloads/pdf/publications/KNOW-YOUR-RIGHTS-PAMPHLET-HIGH-RES-4-11-2025.pdf
- **[V]** Scams and elder abuse, city level: call Aging Connect (212-244-6469) for NYC Aging's Crime Prevention and Support Services (C-PASS). It does safety planning and helps file fraud complaints with the FTC or FBI. https://www.nyc.gov/site/dfta/services/elder-abuse-crime.page
- **[V]** Brooklyn-specific numbers from the Brooklyn DA:
  - Elder Abuse Helpline: 718-250-5299, for victims 60+.
  - Action Center for fraud and financial abuse: 718-250-2340.
  - Source: https://www.brooklynda.org/victim-services-unit/
- **[S]** Adult Protective Services is reached through 311. Call 911 in an emergency. https://www.brooklynda.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Elder-Abuse-Unit-5x7-updated-8-29-23.pdf
- **[!]** The "NYC Elder Abuse Center" is a professional network, not a public hotline. Don't present it as a number to call. (I did not check this with a tool.)
- **[V]** GRIOT Circle: founded 1996 in Brooklyn; the name stands for "Gay Reunion in Our Time." It serves LGBTQ+ elders of color, mostly Black and Caribbean American, most in low-income households. Brooklyn Org lists it as a grantee partner. I found no grant amount. https://brooklyn.org/griot-circle/
- **[V]** VOLS Senior Law Project (formerly the Elderly Project): volunteer lawyers prepare free wills, powers of attorney, health care proxies and living wills for low-income New Yorkers 60+. Hotline: 347-521-5704. https://volsprobono.org/projects/seniorlaw/
- **[V]** VOLS was one of 16 Brooklyn Org grantees in a round of about $1.5M announced around December 2025. https://brooklyn.org/article/brooklyn-org-funds-16-nonprofits-as-government-funding-cuts-impact-services-to-communities/
- **[V]** A health care proxy is a form that names someone you trust to make health care decisions for you if you can no longer make them yourself. https://www.health.ny.gov/publications/1430.pdf
- **[V]** Who can be a proxy: anyone 18 or older. You need two adult witnesses, not a lawyer or notary, and the person you name can't be a witness. https://www.health.ny.gov/publications/1430.pdf
- **[V]** Your doctor can't be your proxy unless they are your spouse or relative. https://ag.ny.gov/publications/advance-directives

## B. Environmental justice

- **[V]** UPROSE (United Puerto Rican Organization of Sunset Park): founded 1966, Brooklyn's oldest Latino community-based organization. Executive director is Elizabeth Yeampierre. https://climatejusticealliance.org/organizations/uprose/
- **[S]** UPROSE's GRID 2.0 Special Purpose District proposal would turn the Sunset Park industrial waterfront into a green, job-creating, lower-carbon district. https://brooklyn.org/uprose/
- **[!]** I couldn't confirm the original GRID plan or that the acronym stands for "Green Resilient Industrial District." Use "GRID 2.0" only.
- **[S]** Sunset Park Solar is a rooftop community solar project on the Brooklyn Army Terminal that serves about 200 families. https://www.adaptationclearinghouse.org/organizations/uprose.html
- **[V]** Peakers are old fossil-fuel plants that run on high-demand days, such as heat waves. DEC says they emit 30+ times the nitrogen oxides (NOx) of newer turbines. https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-moves-to-phase-out-older-peaking-plants-as-it-targets-100-clean-e/549518/
- **[V]** PEAK Coalition members: UPROSE, THE POINT CDC, NYC Environmental Justice Alliance, New York Lawyers for the Public Interest and Clean Energy Group. Its 2020 report was "Dirty Energy, Big Money." https://www.cleanegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/Dirty-Energy-Big-Money.pdf
- **[V]** The DEC Peaker Rule (December 2019) tightened NOx limits in two stages, May 2023 and May 2025. https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-moves-to-phase-out-older-peaking-plants-as-it-targets-100-clean-e/549518/
- **[V]** The grid operator (NYISO) kept the Gowanus 2 & 3 and Narrows 1 & 2 barge peakers running past May 2025 for grid reliability. https://www.utilitydive.com/news/nyc-peakers-planned-2025-retirement-remain-online-reliability-must-run-nyiso/700417/
- **[S][!]** They are reportedly kept until May 1, 2029, the latest date the rule allows. This comes from a secondary summary of NYISO's 2026 planning report; confirm with NYISO. https://www.luminary.energy/articles/2026-nyiso-gold-book-tighter-margins-as-the-system-leans-on-retained-retirements/
- **[!]** I did not check the barges' exact locations in Gowanus Bay and Sunset Park.
- **[V]** Gowanus Canal: proposed as a Superfund site April 2009, officially listed March 4, 2010. https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.cleanup&id=0206222
- **[S]** Cleanup status:
  - Upper canal: dredged and capped. EPA confirmed the capping was finished in a March 30, 2026 letter. https://gowanuscag.org/
  - Middle canal (3rd St to Hamilton Ave): dredging scheduled to run from about fall 2026 to fall 2027. https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.schedule&id=0206222
  - The city is also building two sewage overflow tanks.
- **[V]** NYC Local Laws 60 and 64 of 2017 require an environmental justice study (report), an online mapping tool and a citywide EJ Plan. Local Law 64 also created an EJ Advisory Board and an interagency working group. https://climate.cityofnewyork.us/ejnyc-report/introduction/
- **[V]** The EJNYC Report and Mapping Tool were released April 2024. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/climate/downloads/pdfs/EJNYC_Report.pdf
- **[S][!]** A draft EJ Plan, "EJNYC All In," was posted for public review on November 19. The year isn't on the page but is almost certainly 2026. Comment details are at nyc.gov/ej. https://www.nyc.gov/site/oer/index.page
- **[V]** RETI Center (Resilience Education Training and Innovation), Red Hook: its free "Local Power" program trains people from EJ communities for solar, battery and storage, and building electrification jobs. https://www.reticenter.org/supportworkforce
- **[V]** Community solar: residents share one solar system to lower their energy bills and carbon footprint, without panels on their own roof. RETI runs a sign-up team. https://www.reticenter.org/communitysolar
- **[V]** NYC's Heat Vulnerability Index ranks neighborhoods 1 to 5 on surface temperature, green space, home air conditioning, and the share of residents who are low-income or Black. The city says the gaps are rooted in racism. https://a816-dohbesp.nyc.gov/IndicatorPublic/data-features/hvi/
- **[V]** Caveat for game copy: the city says green space helps cool neighborhoods, but it is linked to heat deaths more weakly than the other factors. Don't make trees the whole answer. https://a816-dohbesp.nyc.gov/IndicatorPublic/data-features/hvi/
- **[S]** Heat contributes to about 500+ NYC deaths a year. Central Brooklyn has among the least tree canopy and highest heat vulnerability. https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/new-york-citys-living-infrastructure-funding-and-managing-nycs-urban-forest-and-tree-canopy/
- **[!]** EPA says shaded surfaces can be 20–45°F cooler. I didn't fetch the page. https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/using-trees-and-vegetation-reduce-heat-islands

## C. Racial justice

- **[V]** The NYC Commission on Human Rights enforces the NYC Human Rights Law. It covers 25+ protected categories in housing, employment and public places. https://www.nyc.gov/site/cchr/law/the-law.page
- **[V]** Some of the protected categories: race, color, religion, national origin, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, immigration status, pregnancy, height and weight. Housing also covers lawful source of income, children in the family and lawful occupation. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/cchr/downloads/pdf/materials/ProtectedClasses_Factsheet.pdf
- **[V]** To report: call 311 and ask for "Human Rights," call 212-416-0197, or use the online form. File within 1 year, or 3 years for gender-based harassment. https://www.nyc.gov/site/cchr/media/source-of-income.page
- **[S]** CCHR hair guidance (February 2019): New Yorkers have the right to natural hair and hairstyles tied to their race, such as locs, cornrows, twists, braids, Bantu knots, fades and Afros. This applies at work, school and in public places. https://natlawreview.com/article/nyc-commission-human-rights-issues-guidance-race-discrimination-basis-hair
- **[S]** NY State CROWN Act, signed July 12, 2019: "race" now includes hair texture and protective hairstyles. New York was the second state after California. https://www.nbcnews.com/news/nbcblk/new-york-second-state-ban-discrimination-based-natural-hairstyles-n1029931
- **[V]** Source of income: since 2008 it has been illegal in NYC for a landlord or broker to refuse vouchers or public assistance, including Section 8, CityFHEPS and HASA. Ads saying "no programs" are illegal too. https://www.nyc.gov/site/cchr/media/source-of-income.page
- **[V][!]** On March 5, 2026, a state appeals court (Appellate Division, Third Dept.) struck down the state-level Section 8 protection. https://decisions.courts.state.ny.us/ad3/Decisions/2026/CV-23-1255.pdf
- **[S][!]** The Attorney General appealed, which reportedly puts the ruling on hold. CCHR says NYC's own law still applies. Check before stating any state-level rule. https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/03/12/voucher-protections-struck-down-nyc-in-limbo/
- **[V]** Spark Prize framing: Brooklyn Org describes the prize as "advancing racial justice." Each year five winners get $100,000 with no strings attached. https://brooklyn.org/sparkprize/
- **[V]** The 2026 winners were B.R.O. Experience, Asiyah, BTFA, S.T.O.P. and YVote, honored March 3, 2026 at Barclays Center. https://brooklyn.org/article/brooklyn-org-reveals-winners-of-the-2026-spark-prize-honoring-five-trailblazing-nonprofits/
- **[S]** Black Trans Femmes in the Arts: founded 2019, it gives Black trans femme artists resources, workshops and funding. The prize will help launch a film production lab. https://www.brooklynpaper.com/2026-spark-prize-spotlight-black-trans-femmes-in-the-arts/
- **[S]** The B.R.O. Experience Foundation: founded 2020 by Barry Cooper, it offers healing, mentorship, therapy and career services for young Black and Brown men in Brooklyn. https://www.brooklynpaper.com/brooklyn-org-spark-prize-2026-b-r-o-experience-foundation/
- **[S]** Asiyah Women's Center: described as NYC's first emergency shelter for survivors of intimate partner violence from Arab, Middle Eastern, Muslim, South Asian and BIPOC communities. It offers advocacy, mental health and housing support. https://www.brooklynpaper.com/2026-spark-prize-asiyah-womens-center/
- **[S]** Weeksville: founded 1838 by James Weeks and other Black landowners, 11 years after New York abolished slavery. It became one of the largest free Black communities in pre-Civil War America and is now in Crown Heights. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeksville,_Brooklyn

Weeksville is only sourced to Wikipedia and NY1 (https://ny1.com/nyc/brooklyn/news/2016/02/24/one-of-nation-s-first-free-black-communities-was-built-in-brooklyn). For game copy, swap in weeksvillesociety.org.
